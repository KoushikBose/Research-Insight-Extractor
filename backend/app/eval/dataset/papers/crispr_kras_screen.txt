Genome-Wide CRISPR Screen Identifies Synthetic Lethal Dependencies in KRAS-Mutant Pancreatic Cancer Cell Lines
Authors: Sofia Alvarez; Peter Lindqvist; Yuki Tanaka
Published: 2024-06-20
Keywords: CRISPR; KRAS; pancreatic cancer; synthetic lethality; Cas9

Abstract
KRAS-mutant pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma remains difficult to treat. To identify genetic dependencies, we performed an in vitro genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 knockout screen in four KRAS-mutant pancreatic cancer cell lines. The screen identified SHP2 and SOS1 as selective dependencies, and knockout of SHP2 reduced cell viability by 70% (p < 0.01). These findings nominate the SHP2-SOS1 axis as a therapeutic target.

Introduction
Oncogenic KRAS mutations occur in over 90% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas. Direct inhibitors exist only for the G12C allele, which is rare in this disease. Synthetic lethal approaches may reveal vulnerabilities that can be exploited pharmacologically.

Methods
Four KRAS-mutant pancreatic cancer cell lines (PANC-1, MIA PaCa-2, AsPC-1 and HPAF-II) were transduced with a genome-wide sgRNA library at low multiplicity of infection. Guide abundance was quantified by next-generation sequencing after 21 days. Cell viability following SHP2 knockout was measured by flow cytometry using annexin V staining. Gene-level scores were computed with MAGeCK, and differences were assessed by Student's t-test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction.

Results
SHP2 and SOS1 were the top depleted genes across all four cell lines. Knockout of SHP2 reduced viability by 70% relative to non-targeting controls (p < 0.01). Combined SHP2 and SOS1 loss produced an additive reduction of 85% in viability. Dependencies were not observed in the KRAS-wild-type control line.

Discussion
The data indicate that KRAS-mutant pancreatic cells depend on upstream signalling through SHP2 and SOS1. A limitation is the use of two-dimensional culture, which may not reflect tumour microenvironment effects. Validation in xenograft models is planned.

Conclusion
The SHP2-SOS1 axis is a selective dependency in KRAS-mutant pancreatic cancer cell lines and a candidate for combination therapy.
